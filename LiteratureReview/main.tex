\section{Three Core Methods}

\subsection{OpenPose: Real-time Multi-person 2D Pose via PAFs}
% OWNER: Yiqiao | STATUS: drafted (kept verbatim from initial version)
The challenges of real-time multi-person 2D estimation lie in the significant
uncertainty caused by the unknown number of people in the image, spatial
interference due to interactions and overlaps between people, and the
substantial increase in spatial complexity as the number of people increases.
Cao et al.'s Part Affinity Fields (PAFs)~\cite{cao2019openpose} effectively
address these issues. Compared to traditional top-down methods (reusing
single-person pose assessment techniques) and traditional bottom-up techniques,
they achieve efficient identification and reconstruction of unstructured
pairwise relationships for a variable number of human body parts through PAFs
(a set of $2\mathrm{D}$ vector fields) and Confidence Maps (a set of confidence
maps of human body parts)~\cite{cao2019openpose}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.62\columnwidth]{figure1.png}
    \caption{Multi-stage prediction network used in OpenPose.}
    \label{fig:openpose-pipeline}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:openpose-pipeline}, a multi-stage prediction
network is used for convolutional neural networks (CNNs)~\cite{cao2019openpose}.
Based on refining PAF (the degree of correlation between individual human
limbs), Gaussian peak convolution is used to further convolve the confidence
map (the location of individual human parts)~\cite{cao2019openpose}. By
changing the $7\!\times\!7$ convolutional kernel to three consecutive
$3\!\times\!3$ convolutional kernels, the operation count is effectively
reduced from $2\!\times\!7^{2}-1=97$ to $51$~\cite{cao2019openpose}. A
spatially weighted loss handles unlabelled individuals. For multi-person
parsing, Cao et al.\ simplify the bipartite matching to a spanning-tree
skeleton solved by greedy inference~\cite{cao2019openpose}.
